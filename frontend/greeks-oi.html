Reading Gamma Well

1. Never read gamma without OI. Per-contract gamma is scale-free — 0.0004 on a strike carrying 40 lakh OI and 0.0004 on a dead strike plot as the same line, but only one of them can move the market. Switch the metric to Gamma × OI (GEX) to weight it; that is the quantity hedgers must actually trade.

2. The sign matters more than the level. Positive net GEX means hedging flow leans against price — rallies get sold, dips get bought, realised vol compresses and ranges hold. Negative net GEX means hedging flow leans with price, so breakouts run and stops cascade. The same chart pattern deserves opposite trade structures either side of that line.

3. Watch where gamma is being added, not just where it sits. Set the OI pane to OI change / bucket and compare it against the gamma line. A strike whose gamma is high but whose OI is unwinding is a wall that is coming down; one with rising OI is a wall being built. The static book tells you yesterday's story.

4. Gamma is a clock, not a constant. ATM gamma rises hyperbolically into expiry — the same strike carries several times the gamma on expiry morning that it did two days earlier. Load an expiry session from the date filter and compare the ATM gamma line's slope against a mid-week session to see it. That acceleration is why short premium gets closed early rather than held to the bell.

5. Use the ladder to find the pin. Near expiry, price is drawn toward the strike with the largest positive GEX and struggles to leave it. When the peak-gamma strike stops moving bucket to bucket while spot oscillates around it, that is pinning — and it is a range condition, not a trend one.

6. Compare CE and PE at the same rung. Black-Scholes gives a call and a put on the same strike identical gamma, so any gap you see between CE and PE at one rung is a gap in implied volatility — that is the skew, and it is read directly here by switching the metric to IV %.

Limits worth remembering. GEX magnitudes are in the broker's own OI units, so read them comparatively — the sign, the shape and the flip level are meaningful, the absolute rupee figure is not. Ladder GEX covers only the selected rungs; the strike profile above covers the full near-money chain. And every Greek here is derived from a solved IV, so a thin or stale print produces a gap rather than a number.
