Every Trade
About the times. The engine runs on daily candles: it enters at the session open and exits at a session close, so entries are shown at 09:15 and exits at 15:30 IST. Those are the real session boundaries the fill is modelled at — they are not measured tick timestamps, and no intraday path is simulated between them. A trade closing the same day it opened was a same-session exit. Result is decided on realised P&L after costs, so a trade that went your way but did not cover charges still counts as a loss.
Reading This

Hit rate is the least useful number here. A crossover that buys options is expected to lose more often than it wins; it survives on the winners being bigger. Read payoff (average win ÷ average loss) alongside it — roughly 45% at 2:1 is a working system, while 60% at 0.5:1 is not.

Max drawdown is the number that decides whether you could actually trade it. It is the deepest peak-to-trough fall in account equity across the whole run. A strategy with a good return and a 60% drawdown is one most people abandon at the bottom, which converts a paper edge into a real loss.

"Only on the crossover bar" changes the strategy, not just the count. Ticked, it takes one entry per swing. Unticked, it re-enters every time the previous position closes while the trend still holds — several times the trades, a lower hit rate, and a materially deeper drawdown.

What these numbers are not. Option premiums are modelled with Black-Scholes on realised volatility, because no historical option-chain data source is wired up. There is no bid/ask spread, no slippage beyond the flat cost model, and no check that the strike had liquidity. Treat a good result as a candidate worth testing on real option data, not as a result.
